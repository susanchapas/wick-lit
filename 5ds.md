Wick: Five Ds Field Guide Learning Modules

Micro-learning concept for Direct, Distract, Delegate, Delay, and Document

Core concept
The Field Guide contains five short modules. Each module follows the same flow:

Learn → See it → Practice → Complete

The collapsed module card shows only the strategy name and completion percentage. Opening the module reveals a short definition, a few examples, when the strategy is useful, and a quick practice exercise.

Progress and unlocking
• 25% — Opened the Learn section
• 50% — Viewed examples / when to use it
• 75% — Completed the recognition or multiple-choice question
• 100% — Completed the final practice
When a module reaches 100%, the next practice module unlocks. All five definitions should remain viewable at any time; only the structured practice progression is locked.

Module structure
Each module should be quick: roughly 30–90 seconds.

Learn: Short definition of the strategy and when it is appropriate.

See it: 2–3 plain-language examples of what the strategy could look like.

Recognize: A tiny situation where the user identifies which response matches the strategy.

Practice: The user either chooses the best response or writes one short response of their own.

Complete: Brief feedback, then the module reaches 100% and the next practice unlocks.

1. Direct
Definition: Address the behavior clearly and immediately when it is safe to do so.

Examples: “Please stop.” · “They asked you to stop.” · “That comment isn’t okay.”

Practice situation: Someone repeatedly comments on another student’s body after being asked to stop.

Recognition question: Which response is Direct?

• A. “Hey, come help me grab something from the kitchen.”
• B. “They asked you to stop. Leave them alone.”
• C. Find the host and ask them to step in.
Correct concept: B — directly names the behavior and asks for it to stop.

Final practice: Write one clear sentence you could say directly.

2. Distract
Definition: Create a diversion that interrupts the situation without directly confronting the person causing harm.

Examples: “Hey, I’ve been looking for you.” · “Can you help me with something?”

Practice situation: Your friend is being pressured to leave a party with someone they just met.

Recognition question: Which response uses Distract?

• A. “Leave her alone.”
• B. Tell the host what is happening.
• C. “Maya, your roommate is looking everywhere for you. Come with me.”
Correct concept: C — gives Maya a natural reason to leave without direct confrontation.

Final practice: Write your own distraction or exit line.

3. Delegate
Definition: Get another person who may be better positioned to help.

Possible people: friend, RA, bartender, professor, event staff, security.

Practice situation: A student on a train keeps following another passenger after she changes seats. You do not feel safe confronting him directly.

Recognition question: What could you do?

• A. Ignore it because direct confrontation feels unsafe.
• B. Tell the conductor or transit staff what is happening.
• C. Start arguing with him.
Correct concept: B — brings in someone who can help while protecting your own safety.

Final practice: Identify who you could delegate to in the situation.

4. Delay
Definition: Check in with someone after the immediate moment has passed.

Examples: “Are you okay?” · “Do you want me to stay with you?” · “Would you like help finding your friends?”

Practice situation: You see a classmate being mocked during a group project. The conversation ends before you feel comfortable stepping in.

Recognition question: What would Delay look like?

• A. Message them afterward: “Hey, that wasn’t okay. Are you doing alright?”
• B. Pretend you did not notice.
• C. Post about the situation publicly.
Correct concept: A — checks in afterward and gives the person control over what support they want.

Final practice: Write one thing you could say when checking in.

5. Document
Definition: Preserve useful information about what happened while keeping consent and privacy in mind.

Practice situation: Someone posts a private image of another student in a group chat without their consent.

Recognition question: Which response best demonstrates Document?

• A. Forward the image to a friend as proof.
• B. Preserve only the information needed to report what happened without spreading the image further.
• C. Repost it publicly to call the person out.
Correct concept: B — documentation should reduce harm, not increase distribution.

Final practice: Identify what information is actually necessary to preserve.
